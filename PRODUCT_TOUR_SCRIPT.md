# Infinity Studio — Page-by-Page Speakable Script

Read this aloud as you click through the app in order, top to bottom of the sidebar. Each section is what to say while that page is on screen. Pause a beat after each one before clicking to the next — let what you just said land before the screen changes.

---

### Intro

"Hi everyone — we're developing Infinity Studio, a platform that turns a single line of text into a fully produced, culturally authentic audio story, in any of eleven Indian languages, not just English. Rather than talk you through slides, let me just walk you through the actual product, the way it's laid out — page by page, nothing hidden."

### Overview

"This is home base — everything the system has generated so far, at a glance. Stories made, characters it remembers, languages live right now. These aren't placeholder numbers on a landing page — they're pulled straight from what's actually sitting in the database. And down here are shortcuts into the four things people use most: dream up a story, build a villain, branch an existing one, or just convert text to speech."

### Audio Studio

"Start simple — this is text-to-speech, eleven Indian languages. Type or paste anything in, and it reads it back naturally. Turn on background music and here's the part that surprises people: it's not a stock loop playing under everything. It reads the mood of what you wrote and generates matching music for it, live, every time."

### Clone voice

"This page works differently from the rest. Instead of picking one of our voices, you hand it a short clip of a real voice, and it narrates your text *in* that voice. It takes longer than the others — thirty to seventy seconds — because it's doing real cloning, not selecting a preset."

### Dream to Story

"This is the one people ask for a second demo of. One line in — a dream, a memory, a scene, whatever — and a whole short story comes out: written, checked for quality, narrated, and given a cover image. And any character it introduces along the way gets remembered, so it can walk into a different story later."

### Villain

"Same machinery, turned toward something more personal: tell it what actually scares someone, and instead of a stock movie monster, it builds a villain and a scene shaped around that specific fear."

### Dub Studio

"And here's the one worth slowing down for. Take any story already sitting in the library, pick a handful of languages, and it doesn't translate it — it *retells* it, once per language, so each version feels like it was written for that culture from scratch. Then it narrates every version, in one pass."

### Stories & Branches

"Nothing generated here gets thrown away — this is the whole library. Pick any story and branch it: describe a decision that plays out differently at some point, and get a new, consistent version from exactly that fork. Switch its language, dub it into several more, or delete it — all from the same card."

### Characters

"Every character who's ever shown up in a story lives here — name, personality, backstory, all remembered. Pull any of them into a brand-new story whenever you want. Same person, different world."

### Quality backlog

"Nothing gets narrated blind. Every story is graded first, on four things: does it sound native, is the tone right, is it culturally accurate, does the emotion actually land. A low score triggers one automatic rewrite — and if it's still weak after that, it lands here instead of shipping quietly."

### Services

"Straightforward — live status of everything this depends on. If something's down, you'll see it here, not guess at it."

### Config

"And this is the system talking about itself — live configuration pulled directly from the running process, not a document that goes stale the week after someone writes it. Chunk sizes, voice settings, which languages have cultural data loaded. What you see is what's actually running right now."

---

### Closing line

"That's the whole product, one page at a time — but it's really one engine doing six things: write it, judge it, speak it, score its mood, remember it, and retell it in as many languages as the room needs."
